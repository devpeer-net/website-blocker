## 2. The opportunity (what the research found)

**Positioning gap — wide open.** Every major competitor stakes exactly *one* axis:

- **Bypass-proof power** — Cold Turkey, FocusMe (hardcore, irreversible locks)
- **Free simplicity** — LeechBlock NG, SelfControl, StayFocusd
- **Gentle tone** — Forest, Focus Bear, Jomo (but freemium, account-based, no cited science)
- **Privacy** — only tiny, unbranded listings (SiteBlocker, "SimpleBlocksite")

**Nobody combines all three of your real strengths:** a *provable* zero-network privacy guarantee **+** real, DOI-cited research delivered at the moment of temptation **+** a calm, non-shaming voice. In two full listicles reviewed (RescueTime, FocusMe), **privacy/tracking/permissions were never mentioned, and neither cited a single real study** — one just gestured at "studies show." The terms **"science-backed," "peer-reviewed," and "gentle friction" are essentially unclaimed territory.**

Even better: the research surfaced that **psychological reactance** theory *validates the design* — rigid blocks you can't undo tend to backfire; friction that preserves your autonomy works better. That's a citable, GEO-friendly angle no competitor names.

**Verified product pillars (from the codebase) that back every claim:**
1. **Provably private** — `declarativeNetRequest` + `storage` only, **zero network calls**, no accounts, MIT-licensed (auditable). Deliberately omits the `tabs` permission.
2. **Science-backed "gentle friction"** — turning blocking *off* shows a real, fact-checked, **DOI-cited** study; the block page does too. 12 dubious claims (the "23-minute refocus" myth, phone "brain drain," goldfish attention span) were **rejected** during fact-checking.
3. **Calm, not punitive** — *"3 sites are out of reach"*, *"a very common pattern, not a personal failing."* No streaks, no shame, no lockout theatrics — you always stay in control.

## 4. Recommended name

**→ Primary recommendation: `Ballast`**

*Ballast* is the weight carried low in a ship that keeps it **steady and upright against the pull of rough water** — a near-perfect metaphor for a tool that quietly keeps you steady against the pull of distraction. It's distinctive, ownable, evocative (the *Forest* lane), and **collision-clear** in the Chrome Web Store.

**Ranked shortlist (all collision-clear in CWS search):**

## 5. ✅ Final copy — ready to paste

### 5a. Extension name (title)

Chrome Web Store indexes the title heavily for search *and* also indexes your summary + description — so a clean, brand-forward title beats keyword-stuffing (which Chrome's newer policy penalizes) and gets better click-through.

**→ Recommended (clean, 35 chars):**
```text
Ballast — Website Blocker for Focus
```

**Keyword-max alternative (53 chars, if you want more search surface):**
```text
Ballast — Website Blocker, Focus & Doomscroll Blocker
```
*Keep the name under ~75 characters, with the brand + "Website Blocker" in the first ~45 (what shows in search results).*

```text
Block distracting websites and get your focus back. No accounts, no tracking—just a calm, science-backed nudge when you're tempted.
```

**Alternative (126 chars):**

```text
Block distracting sites and reclaim your focus. No account, no tracking, no shaming—just a calm nudge backed by real research.
```

## 7. Beyond copy — what else moves the ranking

Copy is ~half of ASO. These compound it:

1. **Icon + 5 screenshots + small promo tile** drive click-through and install rate, which *directly* feed store ranking. Recommended screenshot sequence: (1) the calm block page with a cited study, (2) the "Are you sure?" nudge, (3) the simple blocklist, (4) a "no account, no tracking, open source" trust panel, (5) works-in-Incognito.
2. **First reviews & install velocity** matter a lot for a new listing — plan a soft launch to seed genuine early ratings.
3. **A tiny landing page** (even GitHub Pages) lets you add **FAQPage schema markup** — the store listing itself can't carry schema, so this is where you capture AEO featured snippets and feed GEO engines. Mirror the FAQ above.
4. **Lean into open source for GEO** — a public repo + README carrying this same positioning gives AI engines (which cite GitHub heavily) a second citable source for "private, science-backed website blocker."
5. **Category & single-purpose:** list under *Workflow & Planning* / *Productivity*; the tight single purpose is a Chrome review-approval asset — keep the permission justification (already in your manifest comment) handy.

> **Integrity note — protect your biggest asset.** Your strongest GEO/marketing claim is *provable* privacy ("no network calls, no accounts, open source"). If a future version adds analytics, a paywall, or a `tabs` permission, that claim breaks and the positioning collapses. Guard it deliberately.
